# Original game prompt and design conversation

This document records the original game request and the design clarification answers provided in the conversation. The questions are reproduced from the quoted questions in the user's replies. The initial prompt and answers retain the user's wording.

## Initial prompt

> I'd like for you to create a 3D game that simulates the Stanford GSB Arbuckle Cafeteria (you can search for images online). The player will be a hungry student wandering into the cafeteria and can choose to order food items from various stations. In particular, there should be a food station that serves corn that you can scoop onto your plate, one scoop at a time. After the player is done selecting items, they can then head to the checkout line where they buy the food items from a super friendly Filipino checkout lady named Lilybeth. Please ask me plenty of clarification questions before you start building. I don't care about which graphics engine you choose, but I want the 3D experience to look and feel realistic.

## Design questions and responses

### 1. Where should you be able to play the game?

**Response:**

> In a web browser, easy to launch and share

### 2. What computer will you play on—Mac or Windows, model or approximate age—and will you use a mouse or a trackpad?

**Response:**

> Macbook Pro, we can just do keyboard if its easier. The age range should be all-ages

### 3. Would you like a first-person view, with your plate and hands visible, or a third-person view showing your student character?

**Response:**

> First person, with visible plate and hands

### 4. How closely should the cafeteria match the real Arbuckle layout? If specific entrances, station positions, signs, or seating areas matter, describe them.

**Response:**

> Match the real layout as closely as possible; I can help describe it

### 5. How should scooping corn work?

**Response:**

> Move the scoop into the corn, lift it, and tip it over the plate using the mouse

### 6. Should corn be able to spill, pile up, and overflow, and should portions affect the price?

**Response:**

> Yes to spilling and piling; charge per scoop

### 7. Besides corn, which food stations and dishes are essential? I can start with a grill, pizza, salad bar, and hot-food station. Are there real station names or favorite orders you want included?

**Response:**

> Grill, salad bar, sushi station, asian food station

### 8. What should a lunch visit feel like?

**Response:**

> A small objective: satisfy your hunger within a budget

### 9. How do you picture Lilybeth—approximate age, hairstyle, clothing, and any characteristic greetings or mannerisms? Should her dialogue be written text, voiced, or both? I'll use your description to shape her appearance and warm personality.

**Response:**

> She's probably around 50 with short hair to her chin, wearing a black apron (the Arbuckle cafeteria uniform), and she'll always say, "Sir Mason, I saw Jeff earlier!" You can just have her dialog be written text

### 10. How busy should the cafeteria be?

**Response:**

> Moderately busy, with students dining and a short checkout queue

### 11. What should happen after paying Lilybeth?

**Response:**

> Finish at checkout with a receipt and meal summary

### 12. What audio would you like?

**Response:**

> Mostly quiet, with interaction sounds only

### Follow-up: cafeteria layout

**Question:**

> To help recreate Arbuckle’s layout, describe a walk from your usual entrance to checkout: what’s on your left and right, where are the plates and corn station, where does Lilybeth’s checkout line sit, and where are the seating areas? A rough description is enough.

**Response:**

> I enter from the checkout counter area and the first station is the salad bar which runs perpendicular to the back wall, which features from right to left, the sushi station, the corn scooping station, and the grill. On the right wall is the drink dispensing station and on the leftmost wall is the asian food station
